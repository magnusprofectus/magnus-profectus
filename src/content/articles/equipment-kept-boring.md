Most equipment articles exist to sell you something. This one exists to talk you *out* of buying most things.

**Clothes: whatever you'd wear to move furniture.** Shorts, a t-shirt, socks. Anything stretchy or loose enough not to restrict a squat or a press. There is no performance fabric requirement for 40-minute sessions, that market is selling identity, not function.

**Shoes: almost any trainer works, with one preference worth knowing.** The ideal lifting shoe is flat, thin-soled and incompressible: you want your foot *on* the floor, not cushioned above it, because a squishy sole under a max effort absorbs and wobbles, instability exactly where you want stability. Typical running shoes are the worst offenders (thick compressible heels); normal flat trainers, skate-style shoes or minimalist sneakers are all fine. We train in **barefoot-style shoes (VivoBarefoot)**, flat, thin, wide, and *without* toe separation (the five-toe split-toe look is, let's face it, a bit ridiculous in a gym, and the supposed benefits of individual toe sleeves are marginal), and have had fewer injuries since switching, which is consistent with the stability logic above. It's a preference, not a requirement: any thin flat sole captures most of the benefit.

**The Gymboss: the best $15 in fitness.** A small physical interval timer that clips onto your shorts and stays out of the way for the entire session: set it to beep every 27 seconds for lifting, or alternating 60/30 for the track intervals. It is resistant to impact and water, unlike the phone you would otherwise balance on the floor or a bench, and well worth the investment for what it buys: **it takes the phone out of the working set.** Grabbing, unlocking, staring at a screen mid-set is disruptive precisely where disruption hurts most, the partial sets where every rep counts, and without the phone on the floor you also stop racking weights between partials just to reach it; the timer sits at your hip, one press away. Rep counts are easy to remember and log after the set. The same device covers the interval cardio work we recommend for cardiovascular fitness, consistently, without a battery-draining app. The in-app timer remains as backup.

**The home gym that replaces the gym: PowerBlocks + stand + adjustable bench.** A pair of **PowerBlock adjustable dumbbells**, the matching **PowerBlock stand**, and a sturdy **adjustable bench**, that's the whole setup, it fits in a corner or a garage bay, and it covers chest, back, shoulders, arms, quads, hamstrings and glutes across the entire program. We use the **PowerBlock Pro (EXP) series**, adjustable from roughly **2.5–32 kg per hand in ~2 kg increments** in the base configuration, extendable to ~41 kg per hand with the expansion kits, with the rubberised Pro editions operating more quietly (relevant at 6 a.m. in a family house). The stand matters more than it looks: racking between partial sets should be instant and effortless, deadlifting dumbbells off the floor mid-set costs time and bends your back in exactly the wrong moment. Add an adjustable bench (flat + a couple of inclines) and every upper-body angle is covered.

That list, timer, dumbbells, stand, bench, is the entire equipment chapter. Everything else is optional decoration. The program needs one working weight per exercise, a way to change it, and a clock that isn't your phone.
