---
title: "Lose the fat first"
date: 2026-10-12
blurb: "One goal at a time: why aggressive weight loss and serious lifting don't mix, and a tiered fasting approach to get to your starting line."
---

If you want to build muscle but have significant fat to lose first, our advice is unambiguous: **lose the fat first, then start the program.** Not both at once.

**Why not lift while aggressively losing?** A fast-moving deficit is a terrible environment for strength work: recovery is poor, sessions feel heavy, progression stalls, and stalled sessions, logged truthfully, are demotivating. Trying to chase two goals at once usually means doing both badly and quitting both. Do one at a time. (No, you don't need to add cardio either at this stage, the deficit does the work; cardio is for health and enjoyment, not as the weight-loss engine.) Once you're down near your target body-fat level, you start this program on a **slight caloric surplus with sufficient protein**, and it does what it does.

**How to lose: fasting, tiered by speed.** Fasting works for a simple, well-measured reason, it removes calories without requiring constant willpower at every meal, and trials of time-restricted eating (8-hour windows, no calorie counting) consistently show meaningful weight loss driven by reduced intake, in the 12-month randomized trial, the 8-hour group cut roughly **425 kcal/day** and lost about 4.6 kg, essentially matching traditional calorie counting without counting anything. Choose your tier by how fast you want to lose; each tier adds to the previous one:

- **Tier 1, remove the invisible calories.** Stop drinking calories: water, black coffee, plain tea only. No juices, no milks, no sugary coffees, no sodas, including zero-calorie ones, to be on the safe side (sweeteners are debated for insulin effects; we'd rather not argue with it). And remove all snacks between the three major meals. For many people this alone is a 300–500 kcal/day drop.
- **Tier 2, daily fasting window.** 16/8 (eat within 8 hours) is the easy entry; **18/6** for significant improvement. Eat normal meals inside the window, two or three of them, not a feast compressed into hours.
- **Tier 3, add longer fasts.** The choice within this tier is purely about speed: **24-hour fasts once or twice per week** (dinner to dinner works well) is the moderate setting, skipping a day at a normal 2,000–3,000 kcal maintenance removes that day's intake entirely, and two such fasts a week cut 4,000–6,000 kcal weekly on top of the daily window. **48- and 72-hour fasts once or twice per month** are the aggressive setting, a 48-hour fast removes ≈4,000–6,000 kcal in one act, a 72-hour ≈6,000–9,000. The classic trials back the math: sustained deficits without calorie counting produced ~5 kg (5–6% of body weight) lost over 12 weeks with lean mass preserved, and chamber studies show fat oxidation climbing steadily through a fast (still rising past the 50-hour mark), so what you lose is disproportionately fat. These are advanced tools, not the entry point.
- **Tier 4, do the eating windows as keto.** Keep the fasting structure but make the meals themselves ketogenic: very low carbohydrate, adequate protein, fat filling the rest. Two well-measured effects stack on top of the fasting deficit. First, **appetite**: the meta-analysis on ketogenic diets finds hunger *falls* rather than rising during weight loss, ketosis appears to suppress appetite directly, which is the opposite of what conventional dieting does. In intake terms that's realistically another **300–500 kcal/day** of voluntary reduction. Second, the diet is self-enforcing: with carbs gone, the appetite-triggering glucose rollercoaster is structurally removed, not just managed. Keto also carries a fast **water drop**, each gram of glycogen holds ~3 g of water, and emptying glycogen releases 1–2 kg almost immediately. That's scale drama, not fat, and it comes back with the first refeed, know it before it alarms you.

The rule that makes the tiers work: **the eating windows contain normal meals, not compensation.** A 24-hour fast followed by a 2,500-calorie eating window is theater. This is also why the long fasts deliver more than their raw arithmetic: the same trials that track intake show little or no compensatory overeating on the following days, fast-day deficits mostly *stay* deficits. And the hunger story improves with time, in the eating windows too: with insulin spikes gone quiet, the glucose crashes that drive cravings flatten out, so the meals you do eat satisfy more and chase less.

**The part nobody expects: it gets easier fast.** Hunger and cravings are largely driven by blood-glucose falls following insulin spikes, every snack and sweet drink buys the next crash, and the modern eating pattern is a rollercoaster of them. Introduce fasting and the ride flattens: insulin responses settle, glucose stays stable, and within a week or two most people report cravings dropping dramatically. The 12-month trial data backs the tolerance story, people genuinely live this way for a year without suffering through it. Hunger still visits; it stops being an emergency.

**A rough worked example, all tiers stacked.** Take a common scenario: no liquid calories and no snacks, an 18/6 keto window on regular days, one 24-hour fast per week, except in the two weeks of the month that carry a 48-hour fast, which take that week's place. With a maintenance intake somewhere around 2,200–3,000 kcal/day, the arithmetic lands at roughly a **7,000–8,500 kcal weekly deficit, call it 4–5 kg of fat per month**, plus the 1–2 kg water drop in the first two weeks. Treat that number the way it's offered: as a *very* rough ceiling, not a promise. Real-world results come in below the arithmetic, water fluctuations, eating-window creep, and the body's adaptive responses all erode it, and the deficit shrinks as you shrink (a lighter body burns less). But even at half that rate, the scale would move by double digits across a season, which is why the aggressive tier exists at all.

**A note on candor:** the tiers are tools, not moral grades. Tier 2 held for six months beats Tier 4 attempted for three weeks and abandoned. Get to your target body-fat level, then come back and start the program, and the "when real life gets in the way" article applies just as much to a diet as to training: one goal, held steadily, wins.
