Time is the resource training competes for, and most programs spend it carelessly: long warm-ups per exercise, five sets where one maximal one would do, a commute, waiting for a bench, an hour gone. This program attacks every one of those. Here's the full accounting.

**The main version.** A 2-split, six exercises per workout, run over two to four sessions a week. With the prescribed warm-ups (three ramped sets per exercise) and the prescribed rests, a session is realistically **about an hour, sometimes a little more**, roughly fifteen minutes of warm-up work and transitions per exercise, two to three minutes of working set. That's the true baseline, and at two sessions a week it's already a small claim on your week. Everything below is about trimming the *baseline*, not about surviving an impossible program.

**Modification 1: the warm-up circuit.** The app has a dedicated feature for this: when a session is running, the **"Warm-up circuit"** button opens a single page listing every warm-up set of the session, ordered set 1 of each exercise first, then set 2 of each, then set 3, each with its weight-and-reps recommendation and a checkbox. Work through the list top to bottom, tick as you go, and head into the working sets. (It's unavailable until every warm-up-enabled exercise has a target weight known, enter the weights in the session, or complete one full workout first.) Instead of three ramped warm-ups before each exercise, done conventionally, back-to-back, **rotate them**: warm-up 1 of exercise 1, then warm-up 1 of exercise 2, and so on, then back to warm-up 2 of exercise 1. The weights are light and the reps few, so little rest is needed between them, you're essentially circuiting your warm-up across the session's first exercises. Same total warm-up work, and the idle rest that ramped warm-ups spend mostly waiting shrinks dramatically. A fair estimate for a six-exercise session: the circuit turns **10 to 15 minutes** of warm-up waiting into two or three, and pulls the whole session down toward **45 to 60 minutes**, depending on how briskly you move and how much rest you take between exercises. If you train in a gym, this is the single highest-value modification and it requires nothing but the app.

**Modification 2: the home setup.** The commute is the hidden cost that dwarfs everything in the gym itself. Say the average gym trip is **25 minutes each way**, plus changing, plus waiting for equipment during peak hours (easily 5–15 minutes of standing around), plus the post-workout shower. An hour-long session at the gym is realistically a **2-hour-plus block** of your day.

Run the same session from home, our setup: **PowerBlock Pro (EXP) dumbbells**, the **PowerBlock stand**, and a **generic adjustable bench**, and the arithmetic transforms:

| | Gym | Home |
|---|---|---|
| Commute (both ways) | ~50 min | 0 |
| Changing | ~5 min | 0 |
| Waiting for equipment | ~10 min | 0 |
| Session itself | ~60 min | 40–45 min |
| **Total per session** | **~125 min** | **~40–45 min** |

The home session itself lands at **40–45 minutes** because the two modifications stack: no waiting for equipment at all, transitions measured in seconds, and the warm-up circuit (or simply faster warm-up cycling when nobody is waiting for your bench). At two sessions a week that's **roughly 2 hours saved weekly** versus the gym equivalent, close to **100 hours a year**. The residual benefits don't show up in the table: any time of day works, no peak-hour parking, no monthly fee, no excuses manufactured by weather or opening hours. The "equipment, kept boring" article covers the full shopping list, and the "two workouts, forty minutes, one corner of a room" article is this modification written as our own story.

**What the time is for.** The plain framing: this program doesn't just save time, it *relocates* the cost. The sessions are denser and harder per minute; you're trading comfortable hours for uncomfortable ones. But 40 to 60 hard minutes, at home or with the circuit in a gym, twice a week, that's a version of training that survives careers, children and travel. The time-efficient program is the one you'll still be running in year five.
