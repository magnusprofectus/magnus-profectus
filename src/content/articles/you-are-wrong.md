Someone reading this app disagrees with something in it. That's a statistical certainty, and this article is for you, the reader already composing the objection.

This industry runs on strong opinions. Some of them are attached to good science. Some to bad science wearing good science's clothes. Some methods that genuinely work were developed by, and for, users of performance-enhancing drugs, then marketed to people who are not, which is one reason so many "proven" programs fail the natural trainee so badly. Sorting this stack honestly is hard, and anyone who claims to have it fully sorted is telling you more about their confidence than their evidence.

So let's be clear about our own claims. **We are not claiming other methods cannot work.** There are many ways to skin a cat, and plenty of people have built impressive physiques with conventional sets, conventional rest, high volume, low volume, you name it. If you are happy with the results you are getting from another approach, we are genuinely happy for you: continue with that approach. Nothing here is for people whose training already works.

The critics have a point worth taking seriously. **Most of the great results seen with this method probably do come from the logging.** When you write down every rep of every partial set, every session, and you can see last week's numbers while you lift, you train with an accuracy that almost nobody sustains with a paper plan in their head. A skeptic could reasonably argue that the same detailed logging bolted onto conventional rest periods and conventional sets would capture most of the benefit. That may well be true, and we don't need to fight it, because we then reach the honest question: **why would you not also take the rest of the package?**

Because the combination is the point. Our experience, and that of the users before you, is that the parts work together unusually well: a small amount of time in the gym, one working set per exercise (split into partial sets with short rests for partial recovery), heavy weights moved for a high total number of reps, few sets and few exercises for recovery's sake, no extremely low-rep work, for both muscle size and injury prevention. Each ingredient has a rationale. Together they make a method that is short, heavy, honest and repeatable, which pairs perfectly with the tracking: the log is small enough to sustain forever and sharp enough to force progression. That combination, we would argue, beats the alternatives *for most people*, which is the strongest claim we make, and the only one we need.

And we'll state our biases plainly: you can quite rightly call us hackers of muscle building. We optimize the strength and muscle built per hour, not per year of full-time devotion. This method is aimed primarily at people whose lives contain other things, careers, families, ambitions, and who want the strongest, most muscular version of themselves without paying for it in ten-hour gym weeks. If you love spending countless hours in the gym, chasing skin-tightening pumps or eye-watering weights for their own sake, this program is probably not for you, and the gym will be glad to have you anyway.

So: you may be wrong about us, we may be wrong about some of this, and the industry is wrong about plenty. The log settles it. Run any method you like for twelve honest weeks, write every number down, and the numbers will tell you which of us to believe. Magnus Profectus simply bets that its package, briefly, heavily, accurately, wins that comparison more often than not.
