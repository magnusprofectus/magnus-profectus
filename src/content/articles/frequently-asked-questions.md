Many questions are answered in depth in the other articles here, and we recommend reading those first for anything about the method itself. What follows are the short answers to the questions we hear most.

## Who is this program for?

Anyone who wants bigger and stronger muscles. Muscles all work the same way: they respond to hard work close to failure, then rest and food. If you can train with effort and log your numbers, this program will build you. It is not for people who want the gym to be their social hour; the sessions are short and the work is hard.

## Is one working set per exercise really enough?

The working set is three partial sets stacked on failure, which puts nearly every rep you do in the productive zone. A conventional set spends most of its time far below the intensity where growth is triggered; this method collapses the set so almost every rep is that final, growth-producing fraction. Two sessions a week of that beats an hour of comfortable volume.

## Isn't training to failure dangerous?

Failure is planned here, not stumbled into, and the setup keeps it safe: isolation-friendly exercise selection, dumbbells you can set down or drop, and a timer that keeps you from grinding sloppy reps. The guide has a full section on exercise selection and safety.

## Why 27 seconds of rest?

Long enough to perform the next partial set with real weight, short enough that you have not recovered. That exact tension is the engine of the method. The reasoning is in the guide article on rest.

## How heavy should the weight be?

Pick a weight you can move with good form for the target rep range, then let the app's weight direction handle the rest: beat the total and it suggests going heavier, fall short and it suggests going lighter. You never have to guess.

## How do I progress?

Total load: weight times reps, every session. More reps at the same weight is progress. A heavier weight that keeps you in the rep range is progress. The app marks it with a star; chasing the star is all the program asks of you.

## What if I stall?

Stalling is data, not defeat. One session without progress and the app flags a last chance; two in a row and it recommends a swap; three and it tells you the exercise has done its job and offers a replacement. Every muscle responds to a new movement the way it responded to the old one on day one. Miss a few weeks and the method resets your baseline instead of pretending the gap never happened.

## What about cardio?

Do it. Interval work on a bike, rower or track fits the same short, intense style, and the same cheap interval timer runs both. The guide covers the setup.

## Can I change the program?

Yes. The default program is a recommendation, not a cage: build your own, keep the structure (one working set taken past failure with timed partials, sensible rest), and swap exercises that fit your equipment. The defaults exist so you can start today and learn the method before you customize.

## Do I need the app at all?

No. The method works on paper with a timer. The app carries the arithmetic: warm-up schemes, weight direction, progression and stagnation tracking, so your attention stays on the bar. The pen-and-paper origin is in the guide; the app is what the method deserves.
