title: "Why the name is Magnus Profectus"
date: 2026-10-03
blurb: "Magnus Profectus, Latin for \"great progress\". A name for the only metric that matters, and a reminder that the name, like all names, matters less than the method."
---


The name is Latin. *Magnus Profectus*, "great progress". That's the whole pitch in two words: this app exists to make your progress great, in both senses, large in degree and complete in kind, and to do it in as little time as the method truthfully allows.

Why Latin? Partly because the app's only metric is progression, and the name should say so. Partly because training, like the language, has a long memory: the method this app packages was built by men who studied what worked, measured it, and cut the rest. A dead language suits a method that ignores fashion.

And now the more useful half of the answer: **the name doesn't really matter, and neither, plainly examined, does ours.** Magnus Profectus is not a proprietary discovery. It is a well-tested, conveniently packaged version of **rest-pause training**, a method with decades of use behind it and famous results attached to it. The packaging is ours: the timer done properly, rep targets derived from recovery science, progression tracked, stagnation counted, rotation suggested. The engine underneath, training briefly, heavily, to true failure, and coming back rested, belongs to the method, not to us. If the name vanished tomorrow and the method stayed, you would lose nothing that matters.

So take the name as a label, not a promise. What you should judge is the log after three months: the numbers moving, the mirror changing, the hours spent staying small. If that happens, you made great progress, and the name did its only job.
