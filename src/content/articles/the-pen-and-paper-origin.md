---
title: "The pen-and-paper origin"
date: 2026-09-28
blurb: "This program ran for years in a notebook. The app is the notebook with the arithmetic done."
---

Before this app existed, this program was a small notebook and a cheap interval timer. Every session: date, exercise, weight, three numbers. Every choice, heavier, same, or lighter, was made at the next session by staring at the last line.

It worked better than any app I had used, and I think the reasons are worth keeping now that the notebook is software.

First, the numbers were small. Three numbers per exercise, one line per session. You could see a month of work at a glance, and when progress stopped, it was visible in seconds. Most apps bury the trend under streaks, badges and dashboards. The notebook had one trend: the number, moving or not moving.

Second, the friction was real. Writing a number down takes two seconds and feels like nothing, which meant there was no temptation to log an easy session generously. The app automates the arithmetic but keeps the candor requirement exactly where it was: the number is yours, whatever it is.

Third, the timer was physical. A Gymboss clipped to the waistband, beeping every 27 seconds. No phone in hand mid-set, no screen between you and the last rep. That's why the app still ships with a timer you can ignore, it's a backup, not a leash.

So the app is the notebook with the arithmetic done. It computes the loads, the trends, the stagnation warnings, the exercise rotation, and the warm-up suggestions, all the bookkeeping that made the paper version tedious. What it deliberately does not do is negotiate. It can't talk you out of a number, and it can't stop you from writing down a small one. That part stays with you, same as it always was.
